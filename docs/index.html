Cinema Booking API (1.0.0)

Download OpenAPI specification:

Browse showtimes, hold seats for 15 minutes, and pay for them, without ever selling a seat twice.

A REST API for cinema ticket booking.

  • A booking holds 1 to 10 seats of one showtime for 15 minutes. Paying for it sells the seats; without a payment, the hold expires and the seats are available again.
  • PostgreSQL decides who gets a seat, under row locks taken in one global order. Redis is an accelerator (hold gate, caches, idempotency records, rate limits) that fails open.

Conventions

  • Times are RFC 3339. Showtime times carry the cinema's UTC offset; the timestamps of bookings, payments, and accounts are in UTC. Schedule days start at midnight in the cinema's time zone.
  • Money is in integer cents of the currency named next to it.
  • Languages are lowercase ISO 639-3 codes, such as eng or tha. A showtime has one audio_language and at most one subtitle_language; a client can show them upper-cased, as in "ENG | SUB: THA".
  • Genres are managed by admins. A movie's genres are objects with id, slug, and name; requests refer to a genre by id. GET /v1/genres lists them all.
  • Lists use keyset pagination: pass next_cursor of one page as cursor to get the next. A page without next_cursor is the last one. Cursors are opaque.
  • Errors are application/problem+json (RFC 9457) with type: about:blank. Clients branch on code, which is stable; detail is for humans. Every problem carries request_id, which is also the X-Request-ID response header. A client may send its own X-Request-ID (up to 128 URL-safe characters) to trace a request across services.
  • Request bodies are JSON objects sent with Content-Type: application/json, at most 64 KiB, without unknown fields.
  • Retry-After (seconds) marks answers that are worth retrying unchanged: 409 SEAT_BUSY, BOOKING_BUSY, and IDEMPOTENCY_IN_PROGRESS, 429, and 503.
  • The schedule, seat maps, and the genre list are made for polling: they carry a weak ETag and Cache-Control: no-cache. Send the tag back in If-None-Match, and an unchanged answer is 304 Not Modified without a body. Browsers do that by themselves. The tag depends only on the content, so every API replica gives the same one.
  • Prometheus metrics are not part of this API: they are served on a separate internal port.

Sessions

  • POST /v1/auth/login starts a server-side session. The answer carries a short-lived access token (15 minutes by default) for the Authorization: Bearer header, and sets the session's refresh token in the cinema_refresh cookie: HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict, Path=/v1/auth, and Secure unless the API is configured for plain-HTTP development. Scripts never see the refresh token.
  • POST /v1/auth/refresh trades the cookie for a new access token and rotates the refresh token. Several tabs may refresh at once: within 30 seconds of a rotation, the previous token gets the current one. After that, presenting the previous token again revokes the session, because only a copy of a used token can do that. Every failed refresh is 401 REFRESH_INVALID and deletes the cookie; the client logs in again.
  • A session lasts 7 days after its last refresh, and 30 days after its login at most. A user has at most 20 sessions; a login beyond that ends the least recently used one.
  • GET /v1/auth/sessions lists the caller's sessions, and DELETE /v1/auth/sessions/{sessionID} ends one of them, such as the session of a lost phone.
  • A session that ends before it expires (logout, logout from every session, deleted by its user, a reused refresh token, or evicted by a login over the limit) can no longer refresh, and its access tokens stop working at once: they get 401 INVALID_TOKEN. Should the API lose its Redis, such tokens keep working until they expire, within 15 minutes.
  • The routes that the cookie authenticates accept only a JSON body ({}), so cross-site forms cannot reach them. Every access token names its session (sid); a token without one is 401 INVALID_TOKEN.

Refresh the access token

Trades the refresh token cookie for a new access token, and rotates the refresh token: the answer sets the new one in the cookie. Within 30 seconds of a rotation, the previous refresh token gets the current one and the current one is handed out again unchanged, so that tabs refreshing at once, and a client whose answer got lost, keep their session. Presenting the previous token after that revokes the session.

The body must be the JSON object {}. Limited per session.

Bookings

Seat holds of the caller.

Hold seats

Holds the seats for the caller until expires_at (15 minutes by default). All or nothing: if any seat is taken, nothing is held, and unavailable_seat_ids lists every taken seat of the request.

A caller has at most one unpaid booking per showtime: a second one is 409 ACTIVE_BOOKING_EXISTS, whose booking_id names the first, to continue with or cancel. Seats that the caller's own unpaid booking holds are taken like any other (SEAT_UNAVAILABLE). Limited to 20 attempts per caller and minute. With an Idempotency-Key, a retry gets the first answer again instead of a conflict.

bearerAuth

string (IdempotencyKey) [ 1 .. 255 ] characters ^[\x20-\x7E]+$
Example: 0199a1f0-9e3a-7f4c-9d5a-7b2e4f3a6c99

A key of the caller's choice, such as a UUID. A retry with the same key and body gets the stored answer for 24 hours, with Idempotent-Replayed: true.

Pay for a booking

Charges a pending booking through a payment method and, on success, sells its seats.

The booking must be pending and its hold not over by the database clock. While the provider works, the booking is processing, so the expiry worker leaves it alone and a charge that completes after the deadline still gets its seats.

When the provider does not answer in time, the answer is 202: the payment stays pending, the booking processing, and the worker settles both within a few minutes; follow Location. An Idempotency-Key is required, so a retry after a lost answer gets that answer again instead of paying twice.

The local test provider (development only) takes these tokens: tok_success; tok_slow (succeeds after 5 s); tok_declined, tok_insufficient_funds, tok_expired_card (402); tok_unavailable (503); tok_error and tok_timeout (202).

Schedule a showtime

Puts every seat of the hall on sale: standard and accessible seats at the base price, VIP seats at 1.5 times the base price. The showtime occupies the hall from its start until the movie and 15 minutes of cleaning are over. A hall cannot host two overlapping showtimes; the database enforces that with an exclusion constraint, so two admins who schedule the same slot at once cannot both succeed.

Each showtime is one language version: audio_language is required, subtitle_language is optional. A movie shown dubbed and subtitled is two showtimes. Surrounding space is trimmed from the codes, and they are lower-cased; a code must be the current ISO 639-3 code of a known language (en or the bibliographic fre are refused).
